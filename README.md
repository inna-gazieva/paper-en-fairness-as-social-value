## About

The article proposes a sociological definition of justice as a social value — as distinct
from its traditional understanding as a legal principle, an ethical ideal, or a political
slogan. Drawing on an interdisciplinary synthesis of philosophical concepts of justice
(Plato, Aristotle, Rawls, Höffe) and sociological theories of values (Parsons, Schwartz),
the author applies her four-category empirical model of social value analysis (the Ideal —
the Desired — the Expected — the Real) to survey data from RANEPA students (N = 4338, online
survey "Traditional and Strategic Values of Student Youth", May–June 2023). The study
confirms the integrity of justice as a social value, reveals the high internal consistency
of its components — from the meritocratic ideal to real behavior — and identifies the
"responsibility–justice" link (Spearman's ρ up to 0.718) as the core of the value structure,
with practical recommendations for monitoring value interiorization and value education at
universities.

The empirical model applied here was developed in the author's monograph *Sociology of
Values: Research Methodology* (Moscow: Infra-M, 2024, [doi:10.12737/2133680](https://doi.org/10.12737/2133680))
and in her doctoral dissertation (2025, [doi:10.5281/zenodo.21500892](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21500892)).

## Translation note

This is the author's English translation of the Russian original. The translation preserves
the structure, tables, footnotes, and reference list of the original publication; the
journal's official English title, abstract, reference list, and author information are
reproduced as published. Terminology: «справедливость» is rendered as "justice" in the body
text, in line with the cited philosophical tradition (Aristotle, Plato, Rawls);
«социальная справедливость» as "social justice"; the journal's official English title and
abstract use "fairness". The Russian original is the version of record; in case of any
discrepancy, the Russian text prevails.
